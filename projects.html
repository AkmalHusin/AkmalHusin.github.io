My workflow and tools
  1. Prepare: removed duplicate rows, stripped unit symbols from numeric fields, corrected data types, and reviewed missing values. Filled missing voicemail counts with zero only where the customer had no voicemail plan.
  2. Explore: compared churn proportions and numerical averages; used distribution plots, box plots, and correlation analysis to examine patterns.
  3. Evaluate sampling: explored simple random, stratified, cluster, systematic, and multistage approaches; compared sample churn proportions with the population.
  4. Validate with SQL: stored population and sample tables in SQLite and compared churn percentages using queries.

Interpretation, limitations, and next steps

This is exploratory analysis of the analysed dataset, not a deployed prediction model or a claim of commercial impact. Daytime charges are closely related to usage, so they should not be treated as independent explanations. Service-call averages use available recorded values; missing service-call counts were not treated as zero.

Next steps: compare customers with similar usage, review plan expectations and complaint resolution, and survey both churned and retained groups. Matching a sample's churn proportion alone does not prove that it represents the population across other characteristics.

Public portfolio summary: only aggregate findings and methodology are shown. The source notebook, dataset, individual records, embedded outputs, and training-provider assets are not published.

SQL methods used
  • Explore and filter: inspected table structure and used WHERE, DISTINCT, IN, LIKE, ORDER BY, and LIMIT.
  • Summarise: used COUNT, SUM, AVG, MIN, GROUP BY, and HAVING.
  • Connect tables: explored customer/order and product/supplier relationships through joins, including identifying customers without matching orders.
  • Structure queries: worked with subqueries and views, alongside record-maintenance exercises.

The portfolio charts use read-only SELECT queries. Record-changing exercises from the notebook are not run by this website.

View a verified query

The customer chart is generated from this aggregation:

SELECT Country, COUNT(*) AS CustomerCount
FROM Customers
GROUP BY Country
ORDER BY CustomerCount DESC, Country
LIMIT 5;
Findings and limitations

The top five countries account for 50 of the 92 customer records. Confections contains 13 products, followed by Beverages, Condiments, and Seafood with 12 each.

These figures describe the supplied snapshot, which may contain exercise modifications. They are not presented as the canonical Northwind totals or as results for a real business. No sales-growth or revenue claims are inferred from customer or product counts.
